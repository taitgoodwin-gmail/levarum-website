The name

Relevant, by design.

We needed a word for what we do, and the old ones didn’t fit. “Search” describes a page of ten blue links. “Optimization” describes changing a page to suit an algorithm. Neither describes what happens when a person asks an AI a question and gets one answer back.

The root

So we went to the root. One verb, four words.

Levāre is the Latin verb for raising something up, for lifting a weight. It survives in lever, the tool that moves more than it should. In elevate. And, through its sister word levis, light, in alleviate: to take a burden off. And in relevant, which entered English through Scottish courts in the 1500s as the word for evidence that bore on the matter at hand, the facts worth raising before the judge.

  • lever

    the tool that moves more than it should

  • elevate

    to raise up

  • alleviate

    to take a burden off

  • relevant

    evidence that bore on the matter at hand, the facts worth raising before the judge

    Scottish courts, 1500s

The work

That is what an AI assistant does. One question at a time.

It takes a question, raises the few sources that bear on it, and builds its answer from those. If you’re not among them, the answer goes to someone else.

Levarum is a coined word, built from that root. It’s our name for the work of showing whether a business is relevant in the original sense: raised in the answer because it bears on the question.
